Why This Measure Matters

Comparing life expectancy across wealthy democracies provides an important measure of how successfully nations translate their economic resources into longer lives. Because these countries generally have the financial capacity to invest in quality healthcare, education, sanitation, and social services, differences in life expectancy raise important questions about public policy, health behaviors, environmental conditions, and social inequality.

Such comparisons can help identify countries whose outcomes deserve closer examination and reveal opportunities for improving population health. For citizens and policymakers alike, life expectancy is a clear, easily understood indicator of societal well-being and provides valuable evidence for evaluating the real-world outcomes associated with different policy choices.

Key Finding

America’s life expectancy trails the average of other wealthy OECD countries.

The OECD reports life expectancy of 78.4 years for the United States, compared with an OECD average of 81.1 years—a gap of 2.7 years.

The U.S. is lowest among the 14 wealthy countries in our comparison. Why do Americans live shorter lives than people in so many similarly prosperous nations?

Source: OECD, Health at a Glance 2025
